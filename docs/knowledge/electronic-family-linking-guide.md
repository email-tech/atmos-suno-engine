# Electronic/Synth Family Linking Language for Suno Prompts

Tested, musically correct wording for how electronic and synth instrument families interact: pads, leads, arpeggios, bass, electric piano, organ, drum machines, sampled sources, and vocal synthesis.

Organises all linking on the **plane-of-tone model**: background (beds/pads) → middle ground (color, countermelody, texture) → foreground (leads, hooks, solos).

---

## 1. Electronic families and core roles

- **Synth pads (sustained)**  
  - Sound-source: continuous oscillators, filters, envelopes; sustain indefinitely or for bars at a time  
  - typical role: background harmonic bed, atmospheric foundation, broad emotional space  
  - register: often mid to lower-mid, holding chords  
  - plane: background only

- **Synth leads (melodic, shaped attack)**  
  - Sound-source: pitched synth voices with articulate envelopes, aggressive attack or smooth curve  
  - typical role: main melody, foreground hook, solo lines, counter-melodies  
  - register: wide, typically upper-mid to high  
  - plane: foreground or middle ground

- **Arpeggios (plucked pattern, repeating figure)**  
  - Sound-source: gate-triggered or modulated synth notes in repeating rhythmic shape  
  - typical role: rhythmic texture, middle-ground motion, rhythmic anchor, color motion  
  - register: varies, often mid-to-high for brightbess  
  - plane: middle ground

- **Electric bass (sustained + percussive attack)**  
  - Sound-source: sub, analog, acid, FM, or sampled bass; distinct attack + sustain  
  - typical role: rhythmic/harmonic foundation, low-end groove lock, solo bass lines  
  - register: sub to low-mid, foundational  
  - plane: background/foundation

- **Drum machine (percussive, rhythmic strikes)**  
  - Sound-source: programmed kick, snare, hi-hat, toms; short decay  
  - typical role: rhythmic drive, beat structure, percussive articulation  
  - register: across spectrum (kick low, hats high)  
  - plane: background/foundation (groove)

- **Electric piano / Rhodes (struck + pedal sustain)**  
  - Sound-source: electro-mechanical or emulated struck strings with resonance  
  - typical role: harmonic punctuation, rhythm-keeping, soft melody, solo warmth  
  - register: mid to high  
  - plane: middle ground or foreground

- **Organ (sustained, drawn tone)**  
  - Sound-source: continuous oscillator tone; can sweep filter; drawbar or preset  
  - typical role: harmonic bed, color warmth, rhythmic motion (swell), solo lines  
  - register: wide, often lower-mid for beds  
  - plane: background or middle ground

- **Sampled/chopped sources (varies, often percussive or plucked)**  
  - Sound-source: recorded acoustic or found-sound loops, grains, or one-shots  
  - typical role: textural color, rhythmic anchor, groove pocket, sonic novelty  
  - register: varies  
  - plane: middle ground or rhythm

- **Vocal synthesis / vocal pads / chops (pitched, shaped envelope)**  
  - Sound-source: vocoded or processed human voice; synth-modelled voice; glitch chops  
  - typical role: textural color, lead line, harmonic filler, rhythmic chop  
  - register: varies, often mid for blend  
  - plane: middle or foreground

## 2. Plane-of-tone placement for electronic voices

### Background (Beds and foundations)

- Synth pads hold the harmonic foundation, moving slowly or static for bars  
- Electric bass and drum machine lock the rhythmic/harmonic floor  
- Organ and string-machine pads sit beneath all foreground activity  
- Pad voices are restrained in prominence; they support, not lead  

Linking phrases:
- **pad floats beneath the mix, underpinning the full arrangement**  
- **bass sits deep in the background, locking the groove with the kick**  
- **pad holds a slow-moving harmonic bed while leads move above**  
- **drum machine drives the rhythmic pocket; pad sustains the color**  

### Middle ground (Colour, texture, countermelody)

- Arpeggios weave rhythmic texture between the foundation and foreground  
- Electric piano and organ add rhythmic or tonal motion without leading  
- Sampled color elements add texture and interest  
- Synth leads that do NOT carry the main melody sit here as counter-voices  
- Filter motion and modulation happen in this plane  

Linking phrases:
- **arpeggio pattern locks the groove while the pad holds color beneath**  
- **electric piano threads light chords above the bass pocket**  
- **synth sweep adds textural motion between the rhythm and the lead**  
- **sampled loop sits in the mix, neither leading nor supporting the foundation**  

### Foreground (Leads, hooks, solos)

- Synth leads carry main melodies, hooks, solo lines  
- Prominent vocal synthesis or chops cut through the mix  
- Single, clear voice dominates this plane  

Linking phrases:
- **synth lead sings the main hook over the rhythmic and harmonic foundation**  
- **vocal chop punctuates the groove at the top of the mix**  
- **solo lead floats above the rest of the arrangement**

## 4. Cross-type roles: sustained vs plucked vs percussive

### Sustained voices (pads, organ, held tones)

- Smooth, ongoing harmonic/textural motion  
- Sit in background; provide emotional/harmonic space  
- Enable other voices to dance on top  
- Move slowly or not at all  

Linking phrases:
- **sustained pad holds the emotional tone**  
- **long, breathlike tone underpins the full mix**  
- **pad sustains while shorter, plucked figures dance above**  

### Plucked voices (arpeggios, electric piano, sampled plucks)

- Defined attack, rhythmic shape, clear decay  
- Sit in middle ground; add rhythmic texture and color  
- Lock grooves and rhythmic pockets  
- Interact with the beat  

Linking phrases:
- **plucked arpeggio locks the rhythmic pocket**  
- **electric piano punctuates the phrase with defined attack**  
- **rhythmic plucked figure threads through the arrangement**  

### Percussive strikes (drums, kicks, snares, vocal chops, hits)

- Sharp attack, short decay, rhythmic precision  
- Drive rhythm and create grooves  
- Can sit in background (kick, hi-hat) or middle/foreground (snare, clap, vocal chop)  
- Create emotional impact through placement and timing  

Linking phrases:
- **kick and snare drive the rhythmic foundation**  
- **vocal chop punctuates the beat with a sharp attack**  
- **percussion articulates the rhythm with precision**

## 5. Movement and modulation language (audible behaviour only)

All movement must be expressed as **audible instrument behaviour**, never as production-desk terminology (sidechain, reverb send, automation, etc.). Suno under-renders desk language; describe what the listener hears.

### Filter motion and sweep

- **synth lead brightens as the filter opens toward the chorus**  
- **pad darkens with a smooth filter sweep as energy drops**  
- **filtered texture moves from thin to rich across the phrase**  
- **lead tone opens up with a rising filter curve**  

### Sidechain pumping → Ducking back each beat

- **bass ducks back each beat as the kick lands, then swells forward**  
- **pad breathes in and out with each kick hit**  
- **texture compresses slightly on the downbeat, releasing into the phrase**  
- **pad dips back on the snare hit, creating space for the snare to land**  

### Delay throws and echo trails

- **synth lead throws an echo into the next phrase**  
- **vocal chop trails with delayed repetitions, fading away**  
- **lead synth bounces with rhythmic echo delays**  
- **delayed repeats of the melody blur into the background**  

### Chorus and width modulation

- **pad widens with subtle chorus, creating space and movement**  
- **synth texture expands and contracts with modulation**  
- **lead synth shimmers with a wide, modulated tone**  
- **pad tone broadens with added harmonic width**  

### Distortion and harmonic color shift

- **synth lead gains bite and edge with subtle distortion**  
- **pad tone roughens slightly as distortion kicks in**  
- **texture hardens with added harmonic aggression**  
- **lead tone transforms from clean to gritty, adding intensity**  

### Pitch modulation and glide

- **lead synth bends between notes with a smooth glide**  
- **arpeggio notes glide together, blurring the rhythmic edge**  
- **synth pitch modulates up and down, adding movement**  
- **lead tone sweeps upward, rising with tension**

## 7. Notes on common electronic patterns

**Texture stacking for electronic arrangements:**

- A full electronic build uses **at least three planes** — foundation (bass + drums/pad), middle (arpeggio/electric piano/color), foreground (lead/vocal).
- Omitting any plane creates an incomplete arrangement.
- Overlapping without collision requires **clear plane placement** in your style prompt.

**Suno rendering hints (empirical):**

- Synth pad names alone (e.g., "synth pad") do not guarantee a pad appears. **Describe its placement and behaviour.** Use phrases like "pad sustains beneath the lead" or "pad holds the harmonic foundation."
- Arpeggio and repeating-figure language works reliably when tied to groove. Phrase it as "arpeggio locks the groove" or "arpeggio threads the rhythm."
- Drum machine and kick placement matters. A kick "driving the rhythm" renders better than a kick appearing unnamed.
- Sampled sources need context: "sampled loop sits in the pocket" is more reliable than listing the loop alone.
- Filter motion, width, and distortion **as audible behaviour** render better than desk terminology.
